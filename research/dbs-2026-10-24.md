## Investment thesis and questions
- DBS has strong historical profitability, a deposit franchise and growth in wealth management fees.
- Returns of excess CET1 capital can improve reported ROE, but should not be mistaken for operating growth.
- The extra capital-return dividend is **finite**; model it separately from ordinary dividend income.
- Watch CET1, LCR, NSFR, deposit and loan growth, net interest margins, provisioning, loan delinquencies, net wholesale debt outstanding and refinancing costs.
- Key downside risks: normalised ROE closer to 14–15%, higher cost of equity, worsening credit quality, and expiring capital returns.

## Approach
Primary: justified P/B = (ROE − g) / (Ke − g), multiplied by book value per share.
Cross-check: ordinary Gordon dividend value plus discounted finite capital-return payments.
Do not add independent model estimates together. The valuation is sensitive to small changes in Ke − g.
